How to use Mathematical Wordplay

A quick way to test whether you really know the words maths exams use, and whether you can define them accurately.

Why definitions matter

GCSE and A Level specifications don’t just ask students to calculate. They ask them to understand and use the vocabulary of each topic: factor, identity, chord, interquartile range and hundreds more. Exam questions often turn on a single word, and in “explain” or “show that” questions, marks go to answers that use the correct term.

Nicknames don’t count. For example, “Z angles” isn’t accepted in place of alternate angles. Knowing the precise word, and exactly what it means, is part of knowing the maths.

For students

  1. Choose a topic and level. Start at your current key stage. Each level includes the words from the levels before it.
  2. Read the word and say its definition out loud, or tick Write my answer first and type it.
  3. Press Reveal and compare your answer with the definition.
  4. Be strict with yourself. Press Yes only if you used the key words. If you missed something important, press Not quite.
  5. At the end, practise just the words you missed until you get them all.

Switch to Meaning, then word to work the other way round: read the definition and name the word. Your best score for each list is saved on your device.

For teachers

  • Starter or plenary: put a topic on the board and have the class write each definition on mini whiteboards before you reveal it.
  • Before a new topic: run the previous key stage’s list to check the vocabulary students should already have.
  • Pairs: one student reads the word, the other defines it, and the first student judges against the revealed answer.
  • Reverse mode: “Meaning, then word” works well as a quick-fire quiz, as it checks recognition rather than recall.
  • Homework or revision: ask students to reach a target score, such as 80%, on a list before the next lesson.

How the levels work

Words are grouped by topic and key stage. KS3 covers Years 7 to 9, KS4 covers GCSE and KS5 covers A Level. Lists build on each other, so Geometry KS4 includes every Geometry KS3 word plus the new GCSE words, and Geometry KS5 adds the A Level words on top. Differentiation and Integration are A Level topics, so they only have a KS5 list. All topics mixes every topic up to the level you choose.

Where the definitions come from

The words are taken from the vocabulary that GCSE and A Level Mathematics specifications expect students to understand and use. The GCSE content is set nationally, so it’s the same across AQA, Edexcel and OCR.

Specifications list the words but don’t give official definitions. The definitions here are written in the precise, exam-style wording used in textbooks and mark schemes. If your exam board phrases something slightly differently, go with your board’s wording.
